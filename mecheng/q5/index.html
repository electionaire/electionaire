Method

  1. Strong form: the differential equation at every point, plus boundary conditions. No integrals, full derivatives on \(u\).
  2. Weighted residuals: multiply by a weight function \(\phi\), integrate over the domain, require it for all \(\phi\). Derivatives stay on \(u\); none on \(\phi\); no boundary term.
  3. Product rule and integration by parts (divergence theorem in 2D/3D) move one derivative from \(u\) onto \(\phi\) and create a boundary term.
  4. Weak form: first derivatives on both \(u\) and \(\phi\), plus a boundary term where natural boundary conditions enter.
  5. Discrete system: substitute \(u^h=\underset{\sim}{N}^T\underset{\sim}{u}\) and the same for \(\phi\). Only matrices remain: \(\underline K\,\underset{\sim}{u}=\underset{\sim}{f}\).
  6. Checking a solution: substitute the candidate into the ODE and into every boundary condition. One failure rules it out.
Essential conditions prescribe \(u\) itself; natural conditions prescribe a derivative or flux and live in the boundary term.
