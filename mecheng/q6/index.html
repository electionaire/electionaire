Method

  1. Lagrange family: a full tensor grid of nodes. Order \(n\) in \(d\) dimensions gives \((n+1)^d\) nodes: quadratic is 9 in 2D and 27 in 3D.
  2. Serendipity family: only boundary nodes, no interior ones. In 2D: 4, 8, 12 nodes for orders 1, 2, 3. The 3D quadratic element has 20.
  3. Kronecker property: \(N_i=1\) at its own node and \(0\) at every other node, and all shape functions add up to 1.
  4. Which node? Every factor marks a line where \(N_i=0\): \((1-\xi_1)\) gives \(\xi_1=1\), \((1+3\xi_1)\) gives \(\xi_1=-\tfrac13\). Cross out every node on those lines; the one left over is the answer.
  5. Confirm: evaluate \(N_i\) at that node. It must equal 1.
1D Lagrange factor: \(\ell_a(\xi)=\prod_{m\ne a}\dfrac{\xi-\xi_m}{\xi_a-\xi_m}\). In 2D: \(N=\ell_a(\xi_1)\,\ell_b(\xi_2)\).
Trap. Node numbers follow the exam's figure: corners counter-clockwise first, then the edge nodes. Check the figure each time.
