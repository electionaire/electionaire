Method · boundary conditions

  1. Write the PDE in divergence form, \(\vec\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol X+\text{source}=0\). The quantity under the divergence is the flux \(\boldsymbol X\): \(\lambda\vec\nabla T\) (heat), \(x_2\,{}^4\boldsymbol C:\vec\nabla\vec u\) (rotor), \(E\,\frac{du}{dx}\) (bar).
  2. Essential condition: prescribes the unknown itself (\(T\), \(\vec u\), \(u\)). The weight function vanishes there.
  3. Natural condition: prescribes the normal flux \(\vec n\cdot\boldsymbol X\). It enters the weak form through the boundary integral.
  4. Homogeneous natural condition: \(\vec n\cdot\boldsymbol X=0\). Insulated, stress-free and free ends are all of this type.
  5. Symmetry plane: whatever crossed it one way would have to cross back by symmetry, so the normal flux is zero: the same condition as an insulated edge.
  6. Use the outward normal of each edge: \(-\vec e_1\) left, \(\vec e_1\) right, \(-\vec e_2\) bottom, \(\vec e_2\) top.

The natural condition is exactly the expression that multiplies \(\phi\) in the boundary integral of the weak form. Derive the weak form and you can read it off.
Trap. A condition on \(u\) is essential even when it is zero. Only conditions on derivatives or fluxes are natural.

Method · weak form in five moves

  1. Weighted residuals: multiply the PDE by a weight function of the same type as the unknown (\(\phi\) or \(\vec\phi\), inner product for vectors), integrate over \(V\), write \(\forall\phi\).
  2. Product rule: \(\vec\nabla\cdot(\phi\,\vec q)=\phi\,\vec\nabla\cdot\vec q+\vec\nabla\phi\cdot\vec q\), or for vectors \(\vec\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\vec\phi)=\vec\phi\cdot\vec\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma+\vec\nabla\vec\phi^T:\boldsymbol\sigma\).
  3. Substitute it to replace the term with second derivatives on the unknown.
  4. Divergence theorem: \(\int_V\vec\nabla\cdot\vec a\,dV=\int_S\vec n\cdot\vec a\,dS\). In 1D: \(\int_0^L\frac{d}{dx}(\ldots)\,dx=\big[\ldots\big]_0^L\).
  5. Reorder: the term with \(u\) and \(\phi\) on the left; loads and the boundary integral on the right; repeat \(\forall\phi\).

    Method · isoparametric element

    1. Shape functions at the point: evaluate \(\underset{\sim}{N}^e(\vec\xi_P)\). At a corner this is 1 for that node and 0 for the rest.
    2. Position: \(\vec x_P=\underset{\sim}{N}^{eT}(\vec\xi_P)\,\underset{\sim}{\vec x}^e=\sum_iN_i\vec x_i\).
    3. Local gradients: \(\vec\nabla_\xi N_i=\frac{\partial N_i}{\partial\xi_1}\vec e_1+\frac{\partial N_i}{\partial\xi_2}\vec e_2\), evaluated at \(\vec\xi_P\).
    4. Jacobian: \(\boldsymbol J=\vec\nabla_\xi\vec x=\sum_i(\vec\nabla_\xi N_i)\,\vec x_i\), so \(J_{ab}=\partial x_b/\partial\xi_a\). Its rows are the arrows \(\partial\vec x/\partial\xi_1\), \(\partial\vec x/\partial\xi_2\) in the drawing.
    5. Interpolate: \(\vec u^h_P=\sum_iN_i\vec u_i\).
    6. Global gradient: \(\vec\nabla N_i=\boldsymbol J^{-1}\cdot\vec\nabla_\xi N_i\), so \(\vec\nabla\vec u^h=\boldsymbol J^{-1}\cdot\sum_i(\vec\nabla_\xi N_i)\,\vec u_i\).
