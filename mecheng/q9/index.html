What happens as h shrinks

Right means smaller h. Rising lines are giants, falling lines are crumbs, the flat line is the survivor.

Method · Richardson extrapolation

  1. Error model. \(u_h=u+C\,h^p+O(h^{p+1})\), with \(p\) the order of accuracy from 9b.
  2. Combine two grids. \(\hat u=a\,u_h+b\,u_{h/2}\).
  3. Two conditions. Keep \(u\): \(a+b=1\). Kill the \(h^p\) error: \(a+b\,2^{-p}=0\).
  4. Solve. \(a=-\dfrac{1}{2^p-1}\), \(b=\dfrac{2^p}{2^p-1}\). For \(p=1\): \((-1,2)\). For \(p=2\): \((-\tfrac13,\tfrac43)\).
  5. Backwards (Nov 2025). Given \(a,b\): \(2^{-p}=-a/b\), so \(p=\log_2(-b/a)\).
  6. No \(p\) given? Read it from the error table: halving \(h\) divides the error by about \(2^p\) (see Q4).
Picture. Plotted against \(h^p\), the computed values lie on a straight line. Richardson extends that line to \(h^p=0\).
Trap. The new value is accurate to \(O(h^{p+1})\), not exact. Nov 2024 extrapolates to 0.750 while the true value is 1.
