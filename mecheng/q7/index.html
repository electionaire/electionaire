Method

  1. Work on the reference domain \((-1,1)\), or the square \((-1,1)^2\) in 2D.
  2. Apply the rule: \(I\approx\sum_k w_k\,g(\xi_k)\). It is just a weighted sum of function values.
  3. Know the small rules. 1 point: \(\xi=0,\ w=2\). 2 points: \(\xi=\pm\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3},\ w=1\). 3 points: \(\xi=0,\pm\sqrt{\tfrac35}\), \(w=\tfrac89,\tfrac59,\tfrac59\).
  4. Check exactness first. \(s\) points integrate every polynomial up to degree \(2s-1\) exactly. If the degree fits, the Gauss result equals the exact integral.
  5. Weights add up to the size of the domain: 2 on the line, 4 on the square, 8 on the cube.
  6. 2D is a tensor product: points \((\xi_k,\xi_l)\) with weights \(w_kw_l\). The 2×2 rule has four points \((\pm\tfrac1{\sqrt3},\pm\tfrac1{\sqrt3})\), each with weight 1.
Trap. \(1-\xi^4\) has degree 4, but the 2-point rule is only exact up to degree 3. So \(I_2=\tfrac{16}{9}\ne\tfrac85\).
